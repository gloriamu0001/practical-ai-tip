## 9. Have the AI Interview the User

A short prompt that makes the AI ask question after question, until both sides understand the plan, uncovers requirements before work starts.

- **Use when:** a plan, design or idea exists only roughly.
- **Implementation:**
  1. Paste the prompt below at the start of the conversation, with the plan or idea.
  2. Describe the problem, not a proposed solution. A proposed solution makes the AI treat the approach as settled and ask only about details. An example pair from a source:
     ```
     BAD: "I want to build an analytics panel for admins with graphs and filters"
     GOOD: "Admins can't currently see an overview of the important data in the app"
     ```
  3. Answer each question. Accept the AI's recommended answer where it fits.
  4. Ask for the questions in numbered rounds, highest-impact first: each round holds every question whose prerequisites are already settled, and a question that depends on an open answer waits for the next round.
     > [!info] Sources disagree
     > One source asks for questions in rounds[^MP-rounds]; another asks for one question at a time[^B97].
  5. If the right words for the goal are missing, say so, and ask the AI to restate the goal in plain terms. A phrasing a source uses:
     ```
     i lack the vocabulary to describe what i want
     ```
  6. For a small task, use the short form: hand over the rough idea, ask for three short questions, and get back one prompt to paste. An official source's version:
     ```
     Turn this rough idea into a stronger prompt: [idea]. Ask me three short questions about the task, context and what a useful result would look like. After I answer, give me one copy-paste prompt. Do not fill missing business facts with guesses.
     ```
- **Asset:** the source's general-purpose version of its "grill-me" prompt:
  ```
  Interview me relentlessly about every aspect of this until
  we reach a shared understanding. Walk down each branch of the design
  tree resolving dependencies between decisions one by one.
  For each question, provide your recommended answer.
  ```
- **Limits:** sessions often run 16–50 questions.
