## 61. Pick the Effort Level by the Job

Effort sets roughly how much reasoning the model spends. Higher effort does more checking and makes more choices on the user's behalf. Pick it by the job, not by habit.

- **Use when:** setting up a task in a tool with an effort setting.
- **Implementation:**
  1. Choose by the job:
     - **low:** sketching, brainstorming, easy changes
     - **medium:** regular day-to-day work
     - **high:** work with many edge cases, or where checking matters
     - **max:** hard work handed off completely
  2. For new work, use this loop: have the AI interview the user on the brief, build on low effort while checking that it got the gist, then verify on high effort.
  3. Raise effort for work with many hidden edge cases. Higher effort reduces failures from missed edge cases, but it does not fix a wrong approach.
  4. Change effort mid-conversation when the job changes.
  5. For high-stakes questions, such as a second opinion on a medical or legal concern, use the most capable model available, on at least high effort.
- **Asset:** the source's table:

  | Situation | Command |
  |---|---|
  | Sketching, brainstorming, an easy change? | `/effort low` |
  | Building a feature, regular day-to-day work? | `/effort medium` |
  | Fixing a bug, or chasing edge cases? | `/effort high` |
  | Handing off something hard, end to end? | `/effort max` |

- **Limits:** higher effort costs more tokens; max effort tends to overthink ordinary tasks; phrases such as "think hard" do not set effort.
- **Requires:** a tool with an effort setting (`/effort` in Claude Code).
