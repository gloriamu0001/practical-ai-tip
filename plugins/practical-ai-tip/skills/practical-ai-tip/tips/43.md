## 43. Let a Fresh Checker Judge the Work

An agent that judges work it has just produced favours it, and a reviewer told only to find problems always finds some. Use a checker with a fresh context and a clear scope, and trust an AI checker only once its verdicts match a person's.

- **Use when:** results must be trustworthy: research claims, rankings, important decisions, large batches.
- **Implementation:**
  1. Have the result checked in a second session, or by a second agent, that did not watch the work being produced. For complex work, a model from a different vendor finds things the first model missed.
  2. Give the checker the brief as its scope, and say what to skip.
  3. Use one checker per kind of failure, with a pass-or-fail verdict. One checker asked to catch many kinds of error at once does worse. For a workflow that runs often, have the checkers grade a sample of runs, and track the pass rate over time to catch a decline.
  4. Before trusting an AI checker, have a person label some examples without seeing which model or option produced each one, compare the checker's verdicts with the labels, and fix its instructions where they disagree. Read the checker's instructions directly rather than relying on the AI's summary of them. Checker models differ: some grade generously and miss critical problems, so compare candidates on the same examples.
  5. To compare many options, judge them in pairs rather than score each alone.
  6. For research or articles, list every factual claim and check each one separately.
  7. For work in several parts, run the broad review once, after every part is done.
- **Asset:** an example request from the source:
  ```
  Verify every technical claim in my blog draft against the codebase.
  ```
- **Limits:** costs more tokens; not for simple tasks or still-abstract plans; critique-and-fix cycles can keep adding material.
- **Requires:** a second session, or an agent that can start other agents.
